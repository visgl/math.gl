Local-frame qualification and GeographicLib benchmark baselines

The C8 increment qualifies a bounded profile of LocalReferenceFrame, poses, velocity orientation and EllipsoidTangentPlane. G7 establishes reproducible benchmark baselines before designing more bulk or output-buffer APIs. It adds no runtime dependency, native installation or mandatory model download.

Numerical evidence​

The same 2,450-row matrix runs in Node, Chromium, Firefox and WebKit:

Independent controls use the stored binary64 Cartesian inputs, not idealized pre-rounding coordinates. A monotone Decimal surface equation selects the unique nearest normal; Decimal linear algebra and Taylor sine/cosine construct expected frames and poses. Tests do not derive their expected values from production round trips. Native comparisons share algorithm families and supplement these independent controls.

Frame shapes include a unit sphere, WGS84 and a 0.5 polar/equatorial ratio at ordinary, 1e-60 and 1e60 axis scales. Origins cover both poles, approaches to both poles, a dateline approach, ordinary directions and heights of -0.05, 0 and 0.25 times the largest axis. Local frames retain their sphere/oblate restriction; tangent controls additionally cover a three-radius (3,2,1) ellipsoid.

The existing P0, G3–G6, allocation, coverage and bundle gates remain unchanged. A separate packaged C8 guard checks ESM/CJS/root exports, strict consumer types, ownership and selective dependencies. Relative to pinned master b9db6472, only the two affected C8 classes have a reviewed numerical-fix allowance of 2,000 minified / 1,000 gzip bytes; unrelated Ellipsoid/geodesic imports keep their existing limits and dependency boundaries.

Accuracy fixes​

  • Frame methods retain their ordinary subtract-first computation for small local offsets. A scaled fallback handles intermediate overflow when the final rigid transform components are finite; genuine output overflow still fails before writing the caller's result.
  • Radial tangent-plane lifting scales the homogeneous point and axes before normalization. A huge finite local coordinate on a tiny ellipsoid can therefore produce its representable surface point instead of failing on an overflowing intermediate ratio.
  • Spherical normal lifting uses compensated, power-of-two-scaled square differences. A one-ulp miss is rejected rather than promoted to contact, while a unique grazing root retains its coordinate accuracy. Near-contact forward projection compensates dot products to avoid creating an artificial miss from an otherwise representable contact.

Conventions and remaining domains​

LocalReferenceFrame follows the geodetic normal, including elevated origins. The legacy localFrameToFixedFrame(ellipsoid, ...) uses the Cartesian ellipsoid-gradient convention at elevated origins. Those are different contracts; comparing them as identical frames would be incorrect.

C++ LocalCartesian retains the longitude supplied to its geodetic-origin constructor even at a pole. An XYZ-only local frame cannot recover that meridian. The math.gl Cartesian inverse convention determines its exact-pole frame instead. Pole bases have independent controls; native comparisons use unique non-polar origins and exclude thin/ambiguous-interior cases that do not share that contract.

This matrix is not arbitrary finite-input certification. Near-silhouette normal lifts now use exact dyadic coefficients and a high-precision integer square root instead of promoting small negative discriminants to contact. The 1,485 controls extend the original sphere/oblate/three-radius grid with (2,2,3) prolate geometry at 1e-60, ordinary and 1e60 scales; this is a sampled grid rather than every shape/scale combination. Their mathematical line is defined by the stored binary64 anchor/basis and local coordinates; exact rational signs and independent Decimal roots qualify that kernel. The 999 public checks cover exact axial silhouettes and off-axis cases away from the one-ulp uncertainty of constructing a rounded basis. Basis creation has the separate existing angular budget; these tests do not promise identical hit/miss decisions for ill-conditioned constructor inputs across runtimes. Spheres retain their orthonormal circle convention, including exact contacts. The rare exact fallback allocates and is slower than ordinary normal lifts. Mutable legacy tangent bases, unbounded flattening/scales, temporal attitude continuity and renderer float32 reconstruction remain outside this profile. No allocation-free pose or universal performance claim follows from reusable output arguments.

Reproducing qualification​

From the repository root:

python3 modules/geospatial/scripts/generate-c8-reference.py --check
python3 modules/geospatial/scripts/generate-c8-contact-reference.py --check
python3 modules/geospatial/scripts/generate-g7-reference.py --check
node modules/geospatial/scripts/qualify-c8.mjs --output /tmp/c8-g7-node.json
node modules/geospatial/scripts/qualify-c8.mjs --browser firefox --output /tmp/c8-g7-firefox.json
yarn check:c8-package

The C8 check regenerates the independent Decimal controls. Native controls are stored and provenance-checked; ordinary CI never builds or downloads PROJ or GeographicLib. Reports retain runtime versions, source/bundle fingerprints, per-row errors and budgets.

G7 benchmark scope​

The recorded Node/native baseline contains 20 operation groups, 11 rotated samples with a 16 ms minimum aggregate window, and sampled heap estimates. It pins the measured implementation commit, Node/V8, CPU, compiler, native driver and workload. This shared-host observation flags high spread in some frame/tangent measurements; use its raw samples and flags rather than inferring speedups from individual ratios. It is an initial diagnostic baseline, not a quiet-host release performance claim.

benchmark:g7 compares identical current workloads against historical and candidate math.gl source/export maps and their explicit sideEffects contracts. The resolver preserves that metadata for virtual historical modules; otherwise unrelated baseline code can be retained and distort selective bundle comparisons. It separates solver/frame setup, direct calls that include line setup, reused geodesic/rhumb lines, caller-owned versus allocated frame outputs, poses, velocity orientation and tangent operations. Both revisions must pass the ordinary overlapping C8 profile and the same native G7 output budgets before timing. An optional native executable must itself pass all 72 stored field-by-field controls via --evaluate before any timing; matching metadata and finite checksums alone do not establish accuracy. Newly fixed boundary cases gate the candidate; the benchmark does not compare valid results against baseline failures.

Samples rotate implementation order, adapt aggregate duration, preserve raw measurements and flag limited windows/spread. Inputs, prepared lines and reusable buffers are outside operation timing. Optional heap sampling runs after all operation timing; it estimates allocations and cannot prove zero allocation.

node modules/geospatial/scripts/benchmark-g7.mjs \
  --baseline-ref b9db6472 --points 1000 --samples 7 --min-sample-ms 4 \
  --allocations --output /tmp/g7-node.json

For an offline same-host C++ comparison, provide the already downloaded, pinned GeographicLib r2.7 archive:

python3 modules/geospatial/scripts/build-g7-native.py \
  --archive /path/to/geographiclib-r2.7.tar.gz --output /tmp/g7-native
node modules/geospatial/scripts/benchmark-g7.mjs \
  --baseline-ref b9db6472 --native /tmp/g7-native \
  --points 1000 --samples 7 --min-sample-ms 4 --allocations --output /tmp/g7-native-node.json

The original C++ caller uses binary64, -O2 and no LTO. Geodesics request the same non-area outputs on both sides; rhumbs use the native Exact implementation. Native process startup, I/O and preparation are excluded by its internal timer. Native processes warm per sample while JS shares a process, so cross-language ratios remain diagnostic. Native allocation counts are not claimed. Frame/pose jobs have historical math.gl baselines, not a purported native pose equivalent.

CI uses a short informational smoke profile, with no speed threshold or native compilation. Longer quiet-host measurements, browser throughput, broader shapes and new bulk API designs remain G7 follow-up work. Prefer measured consumer needs over adding speculative signatures.

The numerical helpers, inputs, oracle and benchmark callers are original MIT math.gl work. Retained Cesium Apache-2.0/MIT and GeographicLib MIT SPDX notices and packaged licenses remain with their implementations; no upstream source is vendored by this increment.

G7 packed-frame follow-up​

The four packed frame methods retain the scalar point/vector distinction, independent physical-error budgets, snapshots and extreme-coordinate fallback. They validate the complete input and preflight extreme batches before writing, so invalid records and true overflow fail atomically even with in-place output. The ordinary path hoists matrix coefficients and reuses caller Float64 storage; its implementation creates no per-point vectors.

benchmark-frame-batches.mjs compares historical and current scalar loops using reused vectors against current packed output on identical inputs. It retains wall time and process CPU time, rotated raw samples, spread flags and optional heap estimates. Adaptive CPU windows prevent scheduling pauses from shortening work; process CPU time includes runtime/background work and is diagnostic rather than wall throughput. No new speed gate or native/browser bulk comparison is claimed. Setups and all independent accuracy checks occur outside timing.

node modules/geospatial/scripts/benchmark-frame-batches.mjs \
  --baseline-ref 00dd3e4b --points 4096 --samples 11 --min-cpu-ms 8 \
  --allocations --output /tmp/frame-batches-node.json

The recorded packed-frame baseline pins the measured source and runtime and exposes noisy samples. Interpret its ratios only for that workload and environment. Existing numerical, bundle, allocation, coverage and performance thresholds remain unchanged.

In the recorded 4,096-point workload, packed median process CPU time is about 17–21% of the current scalar loops with reused vectors. Three operation groups have noisy wall measurements, and one current scalar CPU group is flagged for spread. These observations justify the bounded packed API but do not establish a quiet-host release speedup or a zero-allocation guarantee.
