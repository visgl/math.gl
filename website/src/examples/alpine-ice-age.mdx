Explore global grounded ice sheets on a **globe or seven map projections**, or
switch to the detailed **Alpine glaciers** terrain view. Both modes animate
published reconstruction snapshots and support scrubbing, playback speed and
terrain colors beneath the ice. Playback repeats by default; disable **Repeat animation**
to stop at the present. **Ice age names** adds geographic labels for Wisconsinan,
Weichselian and Würm, changing to Holocene at approximately 11.7 ka. These are
regional names and broad chronological context, not synchronous glacier boundaries.

The global view covers **80,000 years** with [PaleoMIST 1.0](https://doi.pangaea.de/10.1594/PANGAEA.905800),
from [Gowan and colleagues (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-21469-w).
It retains 33 snapshots at 2,500-year intervals from the corrected April 2021
1° grid, using the minimal North American MIS 3 scenario. Grounded ice thickness
and changing base topography drive the ice colors and shorelines. Sea ice and
small mountain glaciers are not included. Choose Globe, Equal Earth, Mollweide,
Robinson, Sinusoidal, Miller cylindrical, Equirectangular or Mercator; math.gl
performs the map transformations. Mercator clips at ±85°.

The Alpine view covers the **Würm glacial cycle**, from **119,000 years ago**,
using [Seguinot and colleagues (2018)](https://doi.org/10.5194/tc-12-3265-2018) and
[version 3 of the PISM simulation archive](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7802275).
It samples the original 2 km grid at 4 km for display and retains all 120 snapshots
at 1,000-year intervals. Rotate the relief, adjust vertical exaggeration or use
the overhead view. Present model bedrock is held fixed; modern place labels
provide geographic orientation.

Both source datasets and their derived browser assets are **CC-BY-4.0**. Thickness
and statistics interpolate between published snapshots, so intermediate ice
margins are visualization rather than additional model output. Terrain-reveal
colors are illustrative. Ice area counts thickness above 10 m; volume sums
thickness times grid-cell area. Global totals use latitude-weighted spherical
areas; Alpine totals use the original model grid's nominal 4 km² cells.

**Günz, Mindel and Riss precede both datasets and are not represented.**
The Alpine and global simulations have distinct sources and time ranges.

The [preparation scripts and provenance](https://github.com/visgl/math.gl/tree/master/examples/alpine-ice-age)
verify source checksums and generate the bundled, compressed display assets.
