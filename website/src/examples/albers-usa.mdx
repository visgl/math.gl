This focused example shows the experimental [`createAlbersUsaMap`,
`getMapLayout`, `fitMapLayout`, and `pickMapLayout`
APIs](/docs/modules/geospatial/discontinuous/). States are clipped at region
boundaries, and the cursor readout resolves geographic coordinates through the
frontmost inset. The example enables the five inhabited U.S. territory insets
with `{includeTerritories: true}` alongside Alaska and Hawaii. Alaska is
displayed at 35% linear scale. The preset's broad geographic masks are not
political borders or exact D3 inset clips. Minor outlying islands are outside
this example's coverage.

Use **View globe** to animate the mapped features back onto a sphere, or scrub
the **Unfold** slider. The mouse wheel and zoom buttons magnify all insets
together; drag to pan. The globe adds a public-domain Natural Earth 1:110m land
layer for context. The transition belongs to this example's renderer; the
static `DiscontinuousMap` API supplies the source coordinates and inset layout.
The map opens without graticules or inset boxes; the layer controls can show
them when inspecting the regional composition.
Unlike [Mapbox's election style](https://www.mapbox.com/election-tiles/albers-usa-projection-style),
which preprojects vector tiles for display in Web Mercator, this example
projects the source polygons in the browser with math.gl. Its territory
arrangement is independent of custom D3 composites.

State boundaries come from [Natural Earth 1:10m](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-cultural-vectors/10m-admin-1-states-provinces/), public domain.
See the [standalone source](https://github.com/visgl/math.gl/tree/master/examples/geospatial/albers-usa) and the [discontinuous projection guide](/docs/modules/geospatial/projection/developer-guide/discontinuous-projections).
