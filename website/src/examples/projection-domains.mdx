# Projection domains and external engines

Applications written against math.gl's projection interfaces can still use external projection
engines: prepare their geometry streams and domain boundaries, then hand the resulting buffers to
a renderer. This example uses D3 for clipping/resampling, math.gl for native GeoArrow streaming,
triangulation and worker execution, and deck.gl to draw the results.

Switch between three projections and three synthetic rasters. Polygon pieces retain their source

This is the math.gl adaptation of [deck.gl #10966](https://github.com/visgl/deck.gl/pull/10966).
The [source and limitations](https://github.com/visgl/math.gl/tree/master/examples/geospatial/projection-domains)
explain bounded target-grid meshing and unresolved inverse singularities. Compare the
[source-grid raster recipe](/docs/modules/geospatial/crs/developer-guide/raster-mesh-preparation)
and [external-engine integration guide](/docs/modules/geospatial/projection/developer-guide/external-projection-engines).
