# Tectonic time machine

import Example from "@site/src/components/tectonic-time-machine";

<Example height="calc(100vh - 140px)" />

Animate continental blocks from **1.8 billion years ago** to **300 million years
into the future**. Scrub the timeline, play continuously, visit Pangaea assembly
and breakup, or switch between a globe and seven map projections. Drag to rotate
or pan; scroll to zoom.

Use the **Data source** selector to choose a published reconstruction:

| Source                                                                   | Historical range | What it shows                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Cao et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gsf.2024.101922)           | 0–1800 Ma        | Continental blocks reaching Nuna (Columbia) and Rodinia        |
| [Müller et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.5194/se-13-1127-2022), default | 0–1000 Ma        | Mantle-frame rotations with Merdith et al. coastline templates |

Geometry and finite rotations load from versioned [deck.gl-data Parquet snapshots](https://github.com/visgl/deck.gl-data/tree/master/earth/tectonic-movements/v1).
Each source keeps its own matching geometry, rotations and reference frame. Switching
sources adjusts the timeline range; the two models are never mixed. CAO2024 is in a
paleomagnetic frame, while MULLER2022 is in an optimised mantle frame, so their longitudes can differ.

CAO2024's [published dataset](https://zenodo.org/records/13628813) by Xianzhi Cao,
Alan Collins, Sergei Pisarevsky, Nicolas Flament, Sanzhong Li, Derrick Hasterok and Dietmar Müller is licensed
[CC-BY-4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). The geometry used here was
verified against that record and is pinned by checksum. Geometry is simplified and
triangulated for display, and rotations are interpolated between 10 Ma samples.
MULLER2022 is also CC-BY-4.0 and uses [Merdith et al. (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.earscirev.2020.103477)
coastline templates; see its [data record](https://zenodo.org/records/13636799) for source terms.

Optional region colors identify present-day regional blocks, not ancient continents.
This is a rigid-block visualization: shoreline evolution, sea level, subduction and
changing plate boundaries are not simulated. Older reconstructions carry greater
uncertainty, especially in longitude.

**Future motion is illustrative, not a forecast.** The two original assembly
scenarios rotate present-day regional blocks toward Atlantic or polar arrangements
by +250 Ma, then hold those arrangements until +300 Ma. They do not reproduce
published Amasia, Aurica or Pangaea Proxima models.

Map coordinates use the [math.gl projection engine](/docs/modules/projection),
including `projectFlatSync` on typed arrays. The [polygon module](/docs/modules/polygon)
cuts rings at the dateline and handles polar closure before projection; deck.gl
renders the globe and Cartesian maps.

Scientific data retains its CC-BY-4.0 terms and attribution. No GPlates implementation,
rotation table or coastline dataset is bundled in math.gl. All devices use the same
Parquet batch stream, selecting only the columns needed for geometry or rotations.
The TypeScript decoder streams selected column chunks through HTTP byte ranges into Arrow batches, with decompression and Arrow conversion in a TypeScript worker instead of WebAssembly.
Playback starts automatically when the initial pose is ready, while the rest of history
loads in 100 Ma windows. It temporarily pauses for missing samples and resumes when
they arrive. Interrupted background loads retain valid poses and retry automatically.
Network access is required; the Parquet decoder is packaged with the example.

Modern terrain follows moving blocks for visual context;
ancient or future mountains, vegetation and ice are not reconstructed. Animated ocean
ripples and specular reflections use luma.gl’s public water material.

The Play / Stop button works directly in the embedded example. Sources and scientific
limits are available in the compact **About this reconstruction** box.

Named timeline chapters include Nuna (also called Columbia), Rodinia, Gondwana, Laurussia, Pangaea and the later
Laurasia / Gondwana breakup. Large centered titles fade in and out with geological
time, including during scrubbing; future titles follow the selected illustrative scenario.
Dates are approximate chapter markers, not exact assembly boundaries. Laurussia's
assembly and its later collision with Gondwana are described by the
[British Geological Survey](https://earthwise.bgs.ac.uk/index.php/Geotectonic_setting_of_Wales).
Gondwana's earlier assembly is described by
[Veevers (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.earscirev.2004.05.002).
Nuna is available with CAO2024; Rodinia is available with either source. Proposed
Pannotia is not labeled because its existence and configuration remain debated.
